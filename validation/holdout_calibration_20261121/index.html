    Held-out comparison designs: calibration (2026-11-21)

    What was checked. Whether the held-out comparisons in evalrx.analysis.holdout_designs give the error rates they claim, on simulated data with a known answer. No model and no real dataset is involved.

    How to rerun. python tools/check_holdout_calibration.py --runs 400 --n-boot 1000 (about one minute on one CPU core). Every number below is that command's output.

    1. False positives when nothing is going on

    Data: 120 groups of 2–10 rows. Rows of a group share a failure tendency (group effect with standard deviation 1.2 on the logit scale). A flag has no effect on the outcome. 400 datasets per cell; the target is 0.050 and the simulation error is about ±0.021.

    The flag varies by Rows treated as independent Group column used
    group 0.223 0.075
    row 0.045 0.050

    When the flag is a property of the group — the common case, since a flag computed from an item's features is shared by the rows cut from it — treating rows as independent rejects four times as often as it should. Using the group column brings the rate to 0.075. That is still above 0.050 by more than the simulation error: the percentile bootstrap is somewhat too narrow at this number of groups.

    When the flag varies row by row within groups, both are on target.

    2. Interval coverage when the effect is known

    Data: 150 groups of 2–8 rows with a group-level shift in the failure rate. 400 datasets per design; the target is 0.950.

    Design True effect Mean estimate Coverage
    two-group 0.200 0.200 0.932
    stratified 0.100 0.101 0.963
    difference in differences 0.250 0.258 0.950
    dose-response (per bin step) 0.120 0.120 0.950

    In the stratified data the flag is three times as common in the stratum that fails more. The unadjusted two-group difference averages 0.274; the stratified estimate recovers the within-stratum effect of 0.100.

    3. Keyword recipes

    Data: 120 clips of 3–7 rows, a note column with one real keyword (present in 60% of failing rows, none of the passing ones) and six filler words that are independent of the outcome. Keywords are chosen on a 60% exploration half, split by clip, and re-tested on the rest with the clip as the group column. 200 datasets.
    Real keyword accepted 200 / 200
    Runs in which any filler word was accepted 8.0%
    Mean share of accepted keywords that were filler 5.6%

    The share of accepted keywords that are filler is the quantity the correction is meant to hold at 5%. It came out at 5.6%, which is within simulation error of the target but on the high side, in line with the slightly narrow grouped interval in part 1. The fillers come in pairs that always occur together, so one chance acceptance usually brings a second.

    What this does and does not show

    It shows the estimators are unbiased on data of the shape they assume and that the error rates are near their targets. Where they miss, they miss on the permissive side: 7.5% against 5% for a grouped two-group comparison with 120 groups, 93.2% coverage against 95%, and 5.6% filler keywords against 5%. A result that only just clears the threshold on grouped data deserves less confidence than the threshold suggests.

    It does not show how the designs behave on real tables, where grouping may be partial or unrecorded, strata may be sparse, and the outcome threshold may itself have been tuned on the data. It also says nothing about the quality of the recipes an explorer proposes — only about how a recipe, once frozen, is re-tested.
