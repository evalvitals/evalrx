    Held-out Confirmation

    evalrx explore --holdout-frac F --holdout-confirm explores one part of a table and re-tests what it found on the rest. This page covers what makes that re-test mean something: which rows count as independent, which comparisons can be re-tested, how many claims are corrected together, and what the report says about the data it was given.

    evalrx explore ./results \
  --outcome-col label \
  --group-col clip_id \
  --holdout-frac 0.4 --holdout-confirm \
  --data-card card.yaml

    Rows that are not independent

    Many tables hold several rows per underlying item: frames cut from one clip, one prompt answered by several models, chunks of one document. Rows of an item resemble each other. Two things go wrong if they are treated as independent.

    • The split leaks. A row-level split puts rows of one item on both sides, so the held-out half contains near-copies of rows the explorer has seen.
    • The intervals are too narrow. A thousand rows from fifty items are closer to fifty observations than to a thousand.

    --group-col COLUMN names the item. The split then sends each group wholly to one side, and every held-out interval resamples whole groups (weighted_bootstrap). confirm_report.json records this under split_meta.group_overlap (the share of held-out rows whose group was explored — zero with a group column) and adjudication.clustered_by.

    The size of the problem is measurable. In the calibration run, a flag with no effect at all was called significant in 22% of datasets when rows were treated as independent and in 7.5% when the group column was used; the target is 5%.

    When no group column is named

    The run looks for one. A column is suggested when it has at least 20 distinct values, at least a fifth of the rows share their value with another row, and rows that share a value agree on the outcome well beyond chance (intraclass correlation of 0.1 or more). The warning is printed, written to the report's caveats, and comes with the measured overlap. It reads like this (the numbers depend on the table):

    WARNING: column 'clip_id' looks like the unit rows were cut from (200 groups;
rows of one group share 51% of the outcome variance) and 91.6% of held-out
rows share a group with an explored row, so the held-out check is not
independent of the exploration. Re-run with --group-col clip_id.

    Only string columns and integer id columns are considered. A float column that repeats is a rounded measurement, not an item.

    --time-col COLUMN names a time column the same way. Without it, a column is taken as time when its name contains date, time, timestamp, month, year, week or quarter as a whole word, ends in _at, or holds ISO dates.

    Comparisons a recipe can ask for

    A candidate signal carries a recipe: an expression over the table's columns, fixed during exploration. By default it is compared as two groups — the outcome among rows the expression flags against the rest. Optional fields on the recipe ask for a different comparison. Every number in them is fixed during exploration and reused unchanged.

    Field Comparison Use it for
    (none) Flagged rows against unflagged rows. A threshold or a composite condition.
    where The same, inside the rows where the condition holds. A finding about one subgroup.
    design: stratified + strata The difference within each stratum, averaged with the strata's sizes as weights. A gap that may only reflect a different mix.
    design: did + modifier The recipe's effect where the modifier holds, minus its effect where it does not. An interaction: "the jump is larger for X".
    design: dose_response + bins The change in the outcome per step up fixed cut points. A continuous signal. 
    {"name": "late_batch_matched", "kind": "expr",
 "expr": "batch == 'late'",
 "where": "task == 'i2v'",
 "design": "stratified", "strata": ["generator", "int(encoder_score * 5)"],
 "expect": {"relation": "abs<", "value": 0.10}}

    Expressions may use column names, comparisons, and/or/not, arithmetic, abs/min/max/float/int/len, has_word(column, "word") and contains(column, "text"). None may read the outcome column, whatever it is called.

    A continuous recipe with no bins cannot be re-tested — there is no frozen threshold to reuse — and is skipped with a reason that says so. A stratum that holds only flagged or only unflagged rows contributes nothing and is counted in details.n_rows_in_one_sided_strata.

    The outcome may be two-valued (effects are differences of failure rates) or numeric (differences of means).

    Stating what is expected

    Without expect, a recipe claims "the effect is not zero". With it, the recipe states the prediction to be tested:

    relation Claim
    ">", "<" The effect is above / below value (use 0 for a direction).
    "abs<" The effect is smaller than value in either direction.
    "nonzero" The default.

    "abs<" is how a control is written: a comparison that should come out small. It holds only when the whole interval lies inside the bound. A control that shows a real difference of nine points against a bound of ten is inconclusive, not a finding and not a pass.

    Each claim ends in one of three states:

    • holds — it survived the correction described below.
    • contradicted — the data show the opposite of a bounded claim (not corrected for multiplicity; "not zero" has no testable opposite and is never contradicted).
    • inconclusive — neither.

    reject on a signal means its claim holds and the claim is one of separation; a control that holds has reject: false.

    One correction for every claim

    Every adjudicated signal makes one claim, and so does every numeric prediction a hypothesis carries. All of them are corrected together with Benjamini-Hochberg at alpha. adjudication.method names what was applied — "BH", or "none" when no claim could be tested — and n_in_family counts the claims. raw_reject on a signal says whether it would have passed alone.

    p-values come from the same cluster bootstrap as the intervals. For a two-group comparison of a two-valued outcome with independent rows, the exact (Fisher) test is used instead, because a bootstrap of a small, cleanly separated sample degenerates: four failures against four passes resample to the same difference every time. details.p_value_method records which was used, and details.small_sample_warning appears when the smaller side has fewer than ten independent units.

    Candidates adjudicated during exploration (sufficient statistics) follow the same rule: paired comparisons share an e-BH family and two-group comparisons share a BH family.

    Hypotheses: what the host checks and what the judge grades

    A hypothesis may carry predictions — numeric statements about named signals:

    {"statement": "Late batches fail more on image-to-video, not text-to-video.",
 "predictions": [
   {"signal": "late_i2v", "relation": ">", "value": 0.10},
   {"signal": "late_t2v", "relation": "abs<", "value": 0.10}],
 "experiment": {
   "data_needed": "200 held-out prompts re-rendered with both encoders",
   "operation": "swap the image encoder, keep everything else",
   "expected_result": "the late-batch gap closes",
   "falsified_by": "the gap stays above 10 points"}}

    The host checks each prediction against the held-out rows and sets host_verdict: supported when all hold, refuted when any is contradicted, inconclusive otherwise, none when there was nothing to check. This needs no model. The judge still grades the hypothesis as a whole, is shown the host's results, and is asked to leave them as given.

    experiment replaces a free-text test plan with four fields. It describes the intervention needed when re-counting rows cannot settle the claim; a plan with no falsified_by is visibly incomplete.

    --judge-backend picks the judge: same (default) follows --backend, so a run on Codex does not also need the Claude CLI. confirm_report.json records the judge under judge, including same_as_proposer. When the judge is, or may be, the model that proposed the hypotheses, the report says so; pass --judge-backend or --judge-model for an independent review. Signal verdicts and host-checked predictions never depend on the judge.

    Representative cases

    For each signal whose claim holds, representative_cases lists three rows per side, chosen nearest the side's median on the outcome and on the columns the recipe reads. They show what the comparison is mostly made of; the most extreme rows would show the cases least like the rest. Each entry gives the row's position in holdout_records.json and its id when the rows have one.

    Keywords in a free-text column

    --text-col COLUMN adds keyword candidates. On the exploration rows, the words most over-represented on each side of the outcome are found (log-odds ratio with a prior, counting rows rather than occurrences). Each becomes a recipe has_word(column, "word") whose expect is the direction it showed there, and is then re-tested on the held-out rows in the same family as everything else.

    Some word always leans one way. The discipline is in the second step: a word has to repeat its direction on rows it was not chosen on, after correction.

    What the report says about the data

    Data card. Four facts decide whether a pattern in a table says anything about the system that produced it:

    Field Question
    unit What is one row?
    extraction How was a row cut from the raw object?
    threshold How was the outcome, or its threshold, set?
    aggregation How are results combined when the system is used?

    Give them with --data-card FILE (JSON or YAML) or --row-unit, --row-extraction, --outcome-threshold and --deployment-aggregation. The answers are printed in the report's caveats and passed to the explorer. Any left unanswered are listed there too.

    Leakage check. A column that almost fully determines the outcome is usually the outcome under another name, or something recorded after it. Such columns are reported in the caveats: a column with few distinct values whose values predict the outcome at least 98% of the time, a numeric column whose rank AUC is within 0.01 of 0 or 1, or — for a numeric outcome — a correlation of 0.98 or more.

    Row limit. --max-rows caps what the host loads (default 2000). When rows are dropped the run says how many, and that the loaded rows are taken from the start of each file and are not a random sample.

    From Python

    from evalrx.analysis.holdout import holdout_confirm, split_records

explore, held_out = split_records(rows, 0.4, outcome_col="label", group_col="clip_id")
# ... explore on `explore`, collect candidate signals with recipes ...
confirm = holdout_confirm(report, held_out, outcome_col="label", group_col="clip_id")

    evalrx.stats.compare(a, b, paired=False, cluster_by=(groups_a, groups_b)) gives the clustered two-group comparison on its own, and tools/check_holdout_calibration.py reruns the calibration.

    Limits

    • The bootstrap interval is approximate, and on grouped data it errs on the permissive side: in the calibration run the false-positive rate with 120 groups was 7.5% against a 5% target, and interval coverage was 93% against 95%. With fewer groups expect it to be worse.
    • A group column has to be named or noticed. Grouping that is not in the table — two rows that came from one source but carry no shared id — cannot be detected.
    • Stratification removes differences in mix across the strata that were named. It says nothing about differences along anything else.
    • A claim that holds on held-out rows is a pattern that repeated. Whether it is a cause is the question experiment is for.
